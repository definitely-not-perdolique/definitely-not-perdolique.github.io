The verb (that is, the verb in the predicate) agrees with the subject in person and number.

I am. You were. He is. etc

The subject in the singular requires the verb in the singular. The subject in the plural requires the verb in the plural
His daughter likes cartoons. She likes cartoons. - His daughter like cartoon. They like cartoons.
It's important to make the verb to agree with the subject and not with the random noun in the predicate or in the attributive phrase.
The can of olives is on the kitchen table. The cans of olives are on the kitchen table.
The choice of the singular or plural form of the verb depends not only on the form of the subject but also on the meaning of the subject. So the subject that is plural in form but singular in meaning takes a singular verb and vice versa.
Physics is cool. People are cool too.

Compound subject
If two subjects are connected with "and" a plural verb is used.
The dictionary and the grammar book are on the writing desk.
If the subject connected by "and" refer to the same person or thing, the singular form of the verb is used
Her friend and adviser accompanies her to important meetings.
If the subject are regarded as a unit then it's the singular verb again.
Strawberries and cream is my favorite dessert.

If the subjects are connected by "or; either...or; neither...nor", the verb agrees in number with the nearest subject.
Either the suspect or the witnesses are lying.
If you have a choice it's better to place the plural subject closer to the verb and use plural form.

If the compound subject stand after the predicate expressed by "there is; there are" the verb usually agrees in number with the nearest subject.
There is a book, a newspaper, and two magazines on the table.
Though some style manuals say that plural form is also ok. Like There are a hawk, an eagle, a dove...

There are two magazines, a book, and a newspaper on the table.

II. Verbs: A Glossary of Terms

/Types of Verb

1) Main verbs
Main verbs, or principal verbs, are notional verbs, i.e., they have full lexical meaning of their own.
# Buy, eat, do, give, go, live, love, make, permit, push, see, study, take, try, understand, write.
Main verbs express an action (give, study, take) or a state (love, see, exist).
They can be characterized from different sides: transitive or intransitive; action verbs and stative verbs; regular or irregular.

Phrasal verbs are verbs that form idiomatic phrases with postpositions (give up; turn off; write down) or with prepositions (go into; look for; take after). Postpositions are usually called adverbs or adverbial particles in English linguistic materials.

2) Auxiliary verbs
Auxiliary verb Be, Have, Will, Do perform grammatical functions and do not have specific meaning. The verb Be helps to form the continuous tenses in the active voice and all of the tenses in the passive voice. The verb Have helps to form the perfect tenses. Both Have and Be are used in the formation of the perfect continuous tenses.

The verb Will helps to form the future tenses. The verb Do helps to ask questions and to give short answers in the simple present and simple past.

The verbs Be, Have, Will, Do, can also function as notional verbs, in which case they have lexical meaning and are used as main verbs.
# Can you do it? (do - perform, fulfill)
# Do what you will. (Will - wish)

3) Transitive and intransitive verbs
Transitive verbs require a direct object.
# She made coffee. He reads books. Take a pen and write a letter.
Intransitive verbs can't take a direct object. They may be used without an object or may be followerd by a prepositional object or by an adverbial modifier.
# She waited for the bus
("waited" is am intransitive verb, so you can't say "she waited the bus", hence the prepositional object "for the bus")

The abbreviations v.t. or vt are used to indicate transivite verbs in dictionaries; v.i. or vi indicate intransitive verbs. Many verbs can be used transitively and intransitively.
# (Transitive "leave") He left his bag. He left a note.
# (Intransitive "leave") He left. He left quickly. He left for London.

4) Action verbs and stative verbs
Action verbs express an action (break, turn, run). Most main verbs are action verbs. Stative verbs (aka nonprogressive verbs) do not express an action (because they express state). Generally, they are not used in the continuous tenses.

Stative verbs include:
Verbs of mental perception
# Know, believe, suppose, understand, remember
Verbs of sense perception
# See, feel, hear, smell

IV. Participle and Gerund

1) Comparison of present participle and gerund
They look the same, but present participle is used in the formation of certain tenses and can also be used in the function of an adjective. The gerund is used in the function of a noun.

Participles, gerunds and their constructions are often translated into Russian with the help of adverbial participles (деепричастий) or subordinate clauses (придаточных предложений), i.e., participles and gerunds may be indistinguishable in Russian translation.

2) Present participle and gerund: Simple forms
Pr part:
# The girl is singing. (present continuous)
# The singing girl is very pretty (adjective)
# The girl singing a song is from my class (attribute)
Gerund:
# Singing is her hobby (noun... subject)
# Her hobby is singing (predicative noun)
# She likes singing (direct object)

3) Present participle and gerund: Compound forms
Compound forms are perfect, passive and perfect passive. Remember that the gerund answers question "what".
Participles:
# Having written my article, I asked the secretary to type it.
# The question being discussed now is rather difficult.
# His task having been finished, he went home.
Gerunds:
# I remember having written several letters before writing the report
# She insists on being told the truth
# He was surprised at having been invited to the wedding reception.

4) Participle and gerund after BE
(a) After the auxiliary verb BE, the participle is part of the tense form. After the linking verb BE, the participle is used a predicative adjective. The gerund after the linking verb BE is used as a predicative nominal.
# Her knowledge of European countries is amazing (predicative adjective)
# Her favorite occupation is traveling in Europe (predicative noun)

(b) The present participle in the function of an adjective may be used after some other linking verbs. The gerund in the function of direct object is used after many notional verbs.
# She likes traveling. (direct object)
# He suggested visiting a museum.

5) Participles and gerunds before nouns

6) Gerund after prepositions
Since gerund functions as a noun it is often used after prepositions
# Ater leaving; before asking a question; on receiving their letter
# Necessiti of going there; importance of being punctual
# To be fond of singing; to be proud of winning the game

Note: After the conjunctions "when" and "while" the present participle is used.
# Be very attentive when reading the terms of the contract.
# We discussed those questions while preparing for the meeting.

7) Participles, gerunds and their constructions
Participles, gerund and their constructions often stand at the beginning of sentences. Gerunds are often found after prepositions.
# Talking with her, I understood that she didn't know about that incident. (participle)
# Talking with her was not easy (gerund)
# Coming home, he found several valuable things missing (participles)
# On coming home he found several valuable thins missing. (gerund coming)
(You see, the preposition makes it more like a noun)
# Having called the police, he checked the door lock again. (participle)
# After calling the police, he checked the door lock again. (gerund)
# Having seen him there before, she thinks that he lives there. (participle)
# She remembers having see him there before (gerund, note that this is an object)
# He left early, saying that he had an appointment (participle)
# She left without saying good-bye (gerund)

8) Set phrases with gerund
(a) IT IS NO USE + GERUND
# It is no use waiting for him
# It is no use asking him for help
To understand more clearly why this is a gerund reconstruct the sentence, replace the formal subject "it" with the real subject:
# Waiting for him is (of) no use.

(b) THERE IS NO POINT + IN + GERUND
# There was no point in asking him about it
# There is no sense in going there now

(c) WE HAD TROUBLE + GERUND
# He had trouble finding a place to live
# She had difficulty getting a visa

(d) BE WORTH + GERUND
# This coat is worth buying
# It's a trifle. It's not worth mentioning

9) GERUND AFTER POSSESIVE FORMS OF NOUNS AND PRONOUNS
This whole construction functions as a noun.
# Tom's leaving so early upset our plans
# We are proud of his winning a prize
# She resented their coming to her birthday party without invitation
# I saluted our winning a prize.
# I like this sofa and object to its being replaced.
# These planes are very noisy, and their flying so low is getting on my nerves

In informal speech apostrophe "s" disappears and personal pronouns re used in the objective case.
# We are proud of Anton winning a prize
# We are proud of him winning a prize
# He was angry at them telling him what to do and what not to do.

V. Gerund

1) Forms of gerund
Simple active: writing
Perfect: having writing
Passive: being written
Perfect passive: having been written

Simple active and passive forms usually express an action that is simultaneous with the action expressed by the main verb.
The perfect form and the perfect passive form express an action that precedes the action expressed by the main verb.
# Asking questions is his hobby. (simple)
# I completely forgot having asked him to wait for me there. (perfect)
# She dosen't like being asked such questions. (passive)
# He could not remember ever having been asked to do such a thing (perfect passive)

2) Perfect forms
The simple active form is often used when we don't need to stress that the action precedes the action expressed by the main verb (provided that context is clear).
# I remember asking her about it
# I don't remember being asked about it.
# He mentioned reading it in a magazine.
# He thanked them for inviting him
# He apologized for losing my book.

3) Passive forms
After the verbs "need, require, want" in the meaning "need cleaning, repairing, improving" and after the adjective "worth", the active form of the gerund is used, no the passive form.
# The house needs painting.
# This lock needs repairing.
# His car requires painting.
# Your coat wants cleaning.
# This book is worth reading

4) Negative forms
Depending on the meaning, the negative particle "not" is placed between the auxiliary verb and the main verb or before the gerund.
# He did not apologize for losing my book
# She doesn't like having to work on Saturdays.
///
# He was fined for not paying his debt.
# He apologized for not having written his report on time.
# She is disappointed at not having been invited.

5) Properties and functions of gerunds
The gerund names an action or state and functions as a noun.
The gerund has some properties of a verb. It can take a direct object
# He likes watching films
And can be modified by an adverb
# He is used to working late.

6) Gerunds and nouns
If it is clear who performs the action expressed by the gerund, the possesive forms are not necessary.
# Thank you for helping me (not necessary to use "your helping")
# Now I understand that going to that pary was a mistake. (my going)

7) Substitutes
Examplex of the use of gerunds in different functions in sentences.
# Your not wanting to go there surprises me.
/ # I'm surprised that you don't want to go there
# They suspect him of having stolen several cars
/ # They suspect that he has stolen several cars.

8) Gerund as subject
# There is no telling what he might do next.
(there is - predicate; gerund telling - subject)

9) Gerund after linking verb BE
It plays the role of a predicative noun.
# Her hobby is writing short stories

10) Gerund as direct object
# She suggested visiting Venice.

11) Gerund as prepositional object after phrasal verbs
Gerunds as prepositional object are used after many phrasal verbs, for example, after "accuse of, agree on, apologize for, approve of, believe in..."
# They accused him of stealing their money

12) Gerund as prepositional object after adjectives
Gerunds as prepositional object are used after many adjectives and paritiples, for example, after "Accused of, afraid of, angry at, engaged in, excited about..."
# I'm tired of arguing.
Note the use of gerunds without a preposition after the adjective "worth, busy"
# She is busy typing letters.

13) Gerund as attribute
Gerunds is the function of attrubutes stand after the nouns that they modify, usually after the prepositions "of, for, in, at". The most frequently used preposition is "of"
The gerund is preceded by "of" after the nouns "advantage, art, chance, cost, custom, danger, fear..."
# He has no intention of marrying her
# A key is an instument for opening a door

Note: prepositions before gerunds
Nouns formed from phrasal verbs usually keep the same preposition
# To insist on buying - insistence on buying
# To object to moving - objection to moving

If a noun is formed from a verb that requires a direct object, the preposition "of" is used before the gerund
# To intend calling him - the intention of calling him.
The choice of a preposition also depends on the context.
# The pleasure of walking; no pleasure in waiting; his pleasure at seeing her.

Note: Gerunds and infinitives as attributes are used after many nouns.
# Possibility of going there; hope of finding them
# Decision to stary here; no need to hurry

The gerund in such constructions is closer to the noun and often sounds like a description of an action (opportunity of visiting); the infinitive is closer to the verb and often sounds like an action (opportunity to visit).
# The idea of talking it over with Tom seems interesting.
/ # It might be a good idea to talk it over with Tom.
# They have a good chance of winning this game (chance as possibility)
# You will have a chance to talk to him (chance as opportunity)

14) Gerunds in set phrases
The infinitive is not used in these cases
# Mike is in charge of inviting guests.
# I had no difficulti finding a place to live
# She had trouble (in) hearing what he said

15) Gerunds in consturctions after formal subject IT
On the whole, infinitives are more common in consrtructions with the formal subject IT.
# It is useless asking him
# It is no use talking about it
(It is no use to talk about it)

16) Gerund as adverbial modifier
Gerunds in the function of adverbial modifiers are used after various prepositions.
# Let's discuss it after eating.
# He passed them without saying hello.
Gerunds as adverbial modifiers are used after the compound prepositions "for the purpose of; on the point of; in the event of; subject to"
# He went there for the purpose of seeing his brother

17) Gerund after "no"
Something is not allowed
# No parking. No littering
Or even impossible
# There is no smoking here
# There was no arguing with him when he spoke in such a tone. ( it was useless to argue with him when he spoke in such a tone)
# There is no denying that this situation really scares me.

18) Gerund as predicate
Generally, the gerund is not used as the predicate. But gerunds are used as predicates in questions beginning with "what about" and "how about" and expressing suggestion, usually in everyday speech.
# What about going to Spain this summer?
# How about helping me in the kitchen?
# How about seeing Tony and Ella tomorrow?

19) Gerunds in compound nouns
Usually, a gerund as the first component of a compound noun indicates for what something is used.
# Drinking water; fishing rod; washing machine
